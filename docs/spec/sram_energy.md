# OpenRAM 1 KB weight-store energy

Applies to `sky130_sram_1kbyte_1rw1r_32x256_8` (OpenRAM, 256 x 32 b, 1RW + 1R, 0.191 mm²), the
weight-set store of every compared macro (8 per macro, one per 4 columns; `rtl/src/wset_sram.sv`).
Machine-readable values: [`sram_energy.yaml`](sram_energy.yaml), generated by
`experiments/e20_sram_energy/range.py`. Do not hand-edit numbers here or in the YAML.

## Why the shipped Liberty number cannot be used

The PDK Liberty (`..._TT_1p8V_25C.lib`) charges one constant 9.589 pJ for every clock edge in
every condition (read, write, deselected). OpenRAM writes such constant tables when it
characterizes with an analytical model rather than SPICE (`vlsida2024openramlibpy`). In OpenSTA,
that means 4 edges x 9.589 pJ = 38.4 pJ per cycle per macro whether or not the macro is accessed
(e13 already replaced this with a per-access charge of 19.18 pJ per word).

The macro's silicon (OR1, `cirimellilow2023openramsky130`) has frequency, voltage and retention
data but no energy data. No published measured energy for this macro was found.

## Method (e20, measured in simulation)

- **Netlist.** The PDK's own transistor netlist of the macro, run in ngspice 45.2 with the sky130
  tt models at 1.8 V, 27 °C, 40 MHz (the G1 clock), with 5 fF on each output pin. The energy of
  each clock cycle is integrated from the Vdd supply, rising edge to rising edge.
- **Access pattern.** Clocked idle cycles (both ports deselected); writes (all, half or no bits
  flipping); single-port reads; and the `wset_sram` set-switch pattern. That pattern is
  back-to-back dual-port reads of consecutive words, which puts it in steady state. OpenRAM
  precharges the bitlines in the cycle after an access, so each access gets the following
  "tail" cycle charged to it. Port-0 and port-1 reads of written words are checked bit-exactly.
- **Array trimming.** The 12 rows that are accessed are kept as individual cells. The other 116
  cells of each column become two m-multiplied instances of the same cell, with grounded
  wordlines and complementary stored values. A 10-cell test gave identical bitline charge for
  `m=10` and for ten separate instances (ngspice honours `m` on sky130 subcircuits). Simulating
  the full 8192-cell netlist was not feasible: the DC operating point alone did not finish in
  30 min.
- **Parasitics.** The PDK netlist is the LVS (schematic) netlist. It has no wiring, and its
  transistors have no AD/AS/PD/PS, so there is no diffusion capacitance. Magic 8.3.489 was used
  to extract the bitcell, replica and dummy cells from the PDK GDS (`e20_sram_cell_caps`). The
  extracted bitcell has 5.4-17x the schematic bitline capacitance (about 1.0-1.4 fF per cell
  per bitline) and 4-5x the wordline capacitance. A hierarchical extraction of the whole macro
  with coupling did not finish within 25 min and was stopped, so the periphery remains
  schematic.

## Range

| per macro, pJ | low (schematic) | nominal (extracted array) | high [EST] |
|---|---|---|---|
| clocked idle cycle (deselected) | 6.87 | 7.06 | 14.1 |
| dual-port read cycle, steady (above idle) | 25.3 | 64.3 | 128.7 |
| = per word read | 12.7 | 32.2 | 64.3 |
| one set switch (8 dual-read cycles, incl. first and tail) | 202 | 514 | 1028 |
| single read, port 0 / port 1 (incl. restore) | 9.6 / 14.6 | 31.2 / 34.1 | 62 / 68 |
| write, half the bits flipping | 14.4 | 36.1 | 72 |
| leakage (µW) | 1.0 | 2.5 | 25 |

- **low.** A strict lower bound: wiring and diffusion can only add switched capacitance.
- **nominal.** Measured with the array exact to its layout and the periphery still schematic,
  so it is itself still low-biased.
- **high [EST].** 2 x nominal for dynamic events (10 x nominal for leakage), assuming periphery wiring and diffusion at most double the macro
  energy. It is never set below the Liberty placeholder of 19.18 pJ per read word; that floor
  does not bind.

## Observations that matter for the macro comparison

1. **The idle clock costs as much as one read word in the schematic case.** With the RTL as
   built, the OpenRAM clocks run every cycle (no ICG), so 8 macros cost ≈ 56 pJ per cycle at
   nominal (8 x 7.06). Logic or RTL that runs more cycles per op pays more of this; see e22.
2. **Read energy is set by the array.** It barely moves between low and nominal for idle cycles
   (6.9 vs 7.1 pJ), but per-word read energy grows 2.5x with layout loads. The placeholder
   per-access value (19.2 pJ per word) lies between low and nominal.
3. **Warm-up failure is a start-up artefact, not a timing limit (e29, T2.9, 2026-11-13).** The
   e20 `warm2` read (first read after the DC operating point, directly after the `warm1`/`warm1b`
   writes) returned 0x242400a5 instead of 0xA5A5A5A5. Replaying exactly that prefix
   (`e29_sram_timing --variant e20warm`) gives the identical word 0x242400a5 at **25 ns and at
   50 ns**, so it does not depend on the clock period. Both re-reads of the same word in the following
   cycles return 0xA5A5A5A5, so the write succeeded and the cell is intact. The 11 checked reads of the
   standard pattern (four writes, then five back-to-back dual-port reads, the first directly after
   the last write, then a single read) are all bit-exact at 14, 20, 25, 30 and 40 ns. The
   `warm2` cycle is the only one that reads the same row on both ports with one word never
   written (random initial content) right after the DC operating point; the exact mechanism inside
   the periphery was not isolated. The energy of the measured cycles is used, as before.

## SRAM-limited clock (e29, T2.9)

Same trimmed m-multiplied netlist as e20 (magic-extracted bitcells, tt, 1.8 V, 27 C, 5 fF
loads), waveform-based. OpenRAM launches `dout` on the **falling** clock edge, and `dout` is
valid **3.3-3.7 ns after the falling edge** in every cycle, independent of the period. The
settle time after the rising edge is therefore T/2 + 3.7 ns:

| period | frequency | settle after rise (max over reads) | slack to next rise | reads OK |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 ns | 25 MHz | 23.7 ns | 16.3 ns | 11/11 |
| 30 ns | 33 MHz | 18.7 ns | 11.3 ns | 11/11 |
| **25 ns** | **40 MHz** | **16.2 ns** | **8.8 ns** | **11/11** |
| 20 ns | 50 MHz | 13.7 ns | 6.3 ns | 11/11 |
| 14 ns | 71 MHz | 10.7 ns | 3.3 ns | 11/11 |

No wordline overlap on port 0 (more than one observed row high at once: 0 ns in every run) and no
failures at any period, including the back-to-back dual-port reads and the first read after a
write. **Verdict: the 40 MHz read at 1.8 V is feasible in this simulation with 8.8 ns of
slack; the simulated limit lies below 14 ns.** This is an upper bound only: the model has
schematic periphery, no routed-wire RC, and an ideal 5 fF output load. The only measured
silicon number is **34 MHz for dual-port reads at 1.7 V** (OR1, `cirimellilow2023openramsky130`),
so 40 MHz is 18 % above the highest verified clock. The SRAM-limited clock is therefore stated
as **34 MHz (29.4 ns) silicon-verified; 40 MHz simulation-feasible, silicon-unverified**.
If the conservative point is wanted, energy per op is unchanged to first order, while
latency and throughput scale with the clock: 39.05 GMAC/s becomes 33.2 GMAC/s, and the 32-element
unit time 26 ns becomes 30.8 ns (see `hw_cost_table.md`). The logic blocks were closed at 14 ns,
so they are not the limit at 29.4 ns.
